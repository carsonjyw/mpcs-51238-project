Design gallery · process log

AI Personal Financial Assistant: 25 landing-page versions

Landing-page explorations for a personal finance product. Users connect bank accounts, brokerage accounts, or both, and AI explains how their money changed. Every version uses clearly labeled mock data. No real account connections are implemented.

Versions: 6 / 25 Stage: Exploring new directions Final selection: not chosen yet

Versions

Each card shows a screenshot of the page (1440×900), a link to open it, and a short note on the direction and why it was made. Earlier versions are never overwritten.

Stage 1: directions proposed

Before building anything, five substantially different directions were proposed. Each one differs in audience, layout and how it communicates, not only in visual style. You picked which ones to try; the highlighted cards link to the versions that use them.

Decision log

What was decided, and why, based only on our actual discussion. Newest entries are added at the bottom.

  1. Stage 1 · Directions

    Five directions proposed

    Morning Briefing, Pick Your Path, Ask Anything, One Screen Not Six Tabs, and The Terminal.

  2. Stage 1 · Decision → v01

    Direction 5, “The Terminal,” chosen as the starting point

    “I like that people can see what the product does right away through the tables, charts, and AI notes.”

    Your requests for v01: keep the dark layout and the detailed information, but make sure it's easy to read; include bank accounts alongside brokerage accounts, because users should be able to choose either or both; use mock data and follow AGENTS.md.

    How v01 responds: a larger base text size and high-contrast text on the dark background. Gains and losses use ▲/▼ symbols as well as color, and monospace is used only for numbers. A Bank / Brokerage / Both selector changes which terminal panels appear. Bank panels (accounts, cash flow, spending) sit next to brokerage panels (positions, daily P&L, allocation), and the AI notes cover both.

  3. Stage 2 · Feedback on v01 → v02

    Two features added; direction 2, “Pick Your Path,” tried for comparison

    Your feedback after v01: add a switch between light and dark mode, and a calendar of daily investment gains and losses where each day shows the amount and uses color so gains and losses are easy to spot, with mock data. You asked to try direction 2 for v02 to see how it compares with “The Terminal,” to fit both features into that direction, and to keep v01 unchanged for comparison.

    How v02 responds: the hero is a three-card chooser (Bank / Brokerage / Both). The headline, the preview app, the “what you get” list, the AI questions and the comparison table all change with the choice. A Light / Dark switch sits in the header and the demo. The gains & losses calendar appears on the Brokerage and Both paths. Each trading day shows its amount, green or red with darker shades for bigger moves, ▲/▼ and signs for color-blind readability, and gray for closed days. Tapping a day shows the top movers and an AI summary. v01 was not modified.

  4. Stage 3 · Comparing v01 and v02 → v03

    v02 is easier to understand; v01's investment data is clearer. v01's account picker carried forward; direction 4 tried next.

    Your comparison: v02 is easier to understand overall, but the investment data is clearer in v01.

    What you want to keep going forward: the account section from v01 (its demo's account filter): separate bank and brokerage groups, individual account balances, checkboxes, and quick-select buttons. You said these make it easy to see what you have and choose which accounts to include, and asked to carry that idea into future versions.

    Your request for v03: try direction 4, “One Screen, Not Six Tabs,” keeping v02's clarity and v01's detailed account view in mind but exploring a new layout; keep the light/dark switch and the daily gains-and-losses calendar; leave v01 and v02 unchanged.

    How v03 responds: the hero contrasts six scattered mock app windows (bank, card portal, Schwab, IBKR, a broken spreadsheet, a sticky note) with one calm screen, and a “Merge into one screen” button folds them together. The centerpiece is a working “one screen” built around the v01-style account picker. Bank and brokerage groups each show a subtotal, every account has its balance and a checkbox, and Bank only / Brokerage only / All quick-select buttons and a selected total sit at the bottom. Ticking accounts updates the totals, the gains calendar (which recalculates for only the selected brokerages), the positions table (v01-style columns: Day %, Day $, Value), cash flow and the AI notes. A before/after table and plain section headings carry over v02's clarity. v01 and v02 were not modified.

  5. Stage 4 · Feedback on v03

    v03 didn't feel different enough; v04 should change the organization, not just the look

    Your review: v03 still feels too similar to the earlier versions, specifically its overall structure and the order of its sections. It stays in the gallery as part of the exploration.

    Your request for v04: a bigger change in how the page is organized and how it introduces the product, keeping the core product idea but rethinking the layout and how features are presented, using direction 3, “Ask Anything.” Two approaches are proposed for you to choose from before any code is written.

  6. Stage 4 · Decision → v04

    Approach A chosen: the page is a conversation

    Two approaches proposed: (A) the whole page is a guided chat transcript where features appear only as answers; (B) an ask box with a library of question cards grouped by Cash / Investments / Across both.

    “I like the idea of introducing the product through a conversation instead of another dashboard preview.”

    Your requests for v04: keep it simple enough that visitors quickly understand what the product does; use suggested replies to guide them; have the account picker and calendar appear naturally in the conversation; keep answers scripted and the mock data clearly labeled.

    How v04 responds: one narrow column with no dashboard preview, feature grid or section list. A one-line intro and three “what it covers” chips explain the product up front. The AI then asks which accounts to look at (Bank / Brokerage / Both). The v01-style account picker appears inside its reply, and later answers carry the gains calendar (tap a day to ask about it), checking transactions, spending bars, cash flow and holdings. At most three suggested questions show at a time, chosen from the selected accounts. Every reply is labeled MOCK and a “What can't you do?” reply states the limits. View Demo continues the chat full-screen with the picker in a side panel and a scripted text box.

  7. Stage 5 · Feedback on v04 → v05

    v04's question-led introduction works; direction 1, “The Morning Briefing,” tried next

    Your review of v04: you like how it introduces the product through questions, which feels more personal and quite different from the earlier dashboard layouts.

    Your request for v05: explore the newspaper-style idea, with headlines about the user's money, short stories explaining account changes and sample AI summaries, so it feels like reading a personal financial morning briefing. Keep the bank and brokerage options, the light/dark switch and the gains calendar, worked naturally into the editorial layout, with clearly labeled mock data. Keep v01–v04 unchanged.

    How v05 responds: a masthead (“The Ledgerline Daily,” dated Monday, October 5, 2026) with a numbers index row. A front-page lead story's headline changes with the accounts covered. Beside it are an “About this paper” box that explains the product, “The briefing in 30 seconds” (AI summary) and “Sources for this edition,” the v01-style account picker. Below come three short stories with AI notes, Section B (Markets) with the gains calendar where choosing a day prints that day's story and a newspaper-style stock table, Section C (Household) with the checking ledger, spending and saving, and Letters to the Editor answered by the AI. Bank / Brokerage / Both appears as “This edition covers.” View Demo opens a reader with back issues by date and a scripted “write to the editor” box.

  8. Fix · v05 calendar

    Calendar amounts no longer overlap

    Your report: amounts in the v05 calendar were overlapping. You asked for each day's number and gain or loss to fit inside its own cell on narrow screens too, keeping the newspaper style.

    What changed: the overlap was worst in the demo's narrow “Back issues” calendar. Both v05 calendars now size themselves to their own width. Wide calendars are unchanged; medium ones drop the “$” and use slightly smaller type; narrow ones tighten padding and hide the “Closed” label (the dashed border and legend still mark closed days). Each cell also clips anything that could spill over. Checked with no overflow at 1440, 1024, 768, 375 and 320px wide.

  9. Stage 6 · Feedback on v05

    v05 is creative but too dense; all five original directions now tried

    Your review: the newspaper idea is creative, but it doesn't feel very practical. Too much information is packed together, which makes it hard to find what matters. v05 stays in the gallery.

    Next step: with all five original directions tried, you asked for five more approaches that differ from v01–v05 in how the information is organized and how the product is introduced, not just in visual style. You'll choose one for v06.

  10. Stage 6 · Decision → v06

    Approach 1, “Priority Inbox,” chosen

    Five new approaches proposed, each organizing the page differently: Priority Inbox (by importance), Zoom In (by level of detail), The Timeline (by time), Money Map (by how money moves) and One Card at a Time (by sequence). You chose the Priority Inbox for v06.

    How v06 responds: a short page built around one inbox. A one-line AI summary names what's worth a look. Items sit in three groups (Worth a look, Good to know, and All clear, collapsed by default), and each is a single line until opened, when it shows the AI explanation and its evidence: spending vs. your average, the checking ledger, tech exposure, the gains calendar or each position's move. The v01-style account picker sits beside the inbox as a filter, and unticking an account removes its items. Items can be marked as read. Below the inbox, two short sections explain how items are sorted and what the product reads. View Demo opens a list-and-detail inbox with a scripted “ask” box.

  11. Stage 7 · Feedback on v06 → v06-refined

    Keep organizing by importance, but show more of what the product can do

    Your review of v06: it feels a bit too basic. You like organizing the information by importance, but the page needs to show more of what the product can actually do.

    Your requests for this refinement: refine within the Priority Inbox direction; show one important item expanded by default with a useful AI explanation and clear supporting data; give the page more visual hierarchy; make the summaries more specific to the user's accounts; feel simple to use but substantial, without packing everything together like v05.

    How v06-refined responds:

    • Header: “Good morning. 3 things across your 5 accounts are worth a look,” four summary numbers (the “worth a look” count highlighted), and a line showing what was checked (accounts, transactions, positions, trading days; mock).
    • Top-priority item, open by default: tech at 53% across Schwab + IBKR, more than either account shows. It has a three-part AI explanation (What I noticed / Why it matters / Worth asking yourself), a tech-share chart by account, a holdings table with September results, and follow-up questions with scripted answers.
    • Four levels of size: the open item, then medium cards with small charts for the rest of “Worth a look,” compact rows with small charts for “Good to know,” and one line of chips for “All clear.” Opening any item moves it into the large view.
    • Specific summaries: each item names the account (for example “Rewards Card ••3398”), the amounts and the dates.

    The top priority changes with the selected accounts (with bank only, the dining item leads). The gains calendar is the evidence for the September item, and the demo adds a scripted ask box. v06 is kept unchanged for comparison. At your request this refinement is named v06-refined and sits next to v06; v07 is reserved for the next substantially different direction.

  12. Stage 8 · Decision → v07

    Approach 2, “Zoom In,” chosen for the next new direction

    Your decision: keep the Priority Inbox refinement as v06-refined beside v06, use v07 for the next substantially different direction, and make that direction “Zoom In: one number you can open up.”

    How v07 responds: the page starts with a single figure, net worth $118,115, plus one AI sentence. A “What it's made of” bar and tiles break it into Cash and Invested; tapping zooms in to each bank account or brokerage, then to individual holdings (four levels). Each level has its own big number, AI explanation and supporting data:

    • Net worth: September at a glance, plus “worth a look further down” links
    • Cash: spending vs. average, and income vs. spending
    • Checking: a step-by-step balance chart
    • Invested and each brokerage: the gains calendar, tech share and holdings
    • Each holding: its September day by day

    A breadcrumb trail and “Zoom out” button keep navigation simple. The v01-style account picker decides which branches exist, and an arrow on each account jumps straight to it. The demo adds a scripted “find” box (e.g. “NVDA” zooms to that holding).

  13. Stage 9 · Decision → v08

    Approach 3, “The Timeline,” chosen

    Your decision: move on to The Timeline, organized by time.

    How v08 responds: one chronological feed instead of panels or levels.

    • Above the Today marker: a compact “Coming up” card (next paycheck, card due date, savings transfer, rent).
    • Below it: a “since you last checked” summary, then every day with activity, newest first. Bank transactions from Mock Bank sit beside daily market results from Schwab and IBKR.
    • AI recaps: each week and each month closes with an AI recap (investment result, money in and out, anything unusual, such as the skipped September savings transfer).
    • Transfers: moves between your own accounts are labeled as transfers and kept out of the totals.
    • Market days: each one opens to show every position's move.

    The gains calendar sits beside the feed as a scrubber: clicking a day jumps to it, and the calendar follows as you scroll. Blue dots mark days with bank activity. The v01-style account picker filters the feed, “Key moments only” keeps just the flagged events, and “Load August” extends history. The demo adds a scripted “find” box (e.g. “steakhouse,” “worst day”).

  14. Stage 10 · Feedback on v08

    The timeline's combined view and “Since you last checked” work; recent changes should come before upcoming events

    Your review of v08: you like how the timeline brings bank activity and investment changes together, especially the “Since you last checked” summary. You'd make recent changes more prominent and move the upcoming events lower down, so users can quickly see what has happened before looking ahead.

    Next step: you asked to move on to the next new direction, Money Map, for v09. v08 is kept as is.

  15. Stage 10 · Decision → v09

    Approach 4, “Money Map,” chosen

    How v09 responds: the page centers on one flow diagram of September instead of panels, a feed or levels.

    • Left column: money in (paychecks $6,850, market gains +$2,080).
    • Middle: the accounts it went through (Everyday Checking, Schwab, IBKR).
    • Right: where it ended up (spending $5,120, kept $1,730, and a dashed $0 line to savings for the skipped transfer), then spending by category.

    Bands are sized by amount, and items worth a look (dining, the skipped savings transfer) carry an orange dot. Hovering lights up connected flows; clicking any box or band opens a side panel with an AI explanation and its data: dining by month and the weekend meals, the savings balance with the flat September, each holding's contribution, or the gains calendar for Market gains and each brokerage. The panel opens on a September overview with “worth a look” shortcuts. The v01-style account picker is laid out as a bar above the map and decides which flows exist. The demo adds a scripted “find” box (e.g. “rent” highlights Housing).

  16. Stage 11 · Feedback on v09 → v10

    The flow view is clear at a glance; last of the five new approaches tried next

    Your review of v09: you like how it shows where the money comes from and where it goes. The main flows are easy to understand without extra clicks.

    Your request: move on to approach 5, “One Card at a Time,” organized as a short sequence, for v10.

  17. Stage 11 · Decision → v10

    Approach 5, “One Card at a Time,” built

    How v10 responds: instead of one long page, eight full-screen cards that snap into place, each with one idea and one visual:

    1. Intro
    2. Choose accounts (v01-style picker)
    3. Since you last checked, with a big +$1,388
    4. Where the money went, as two simple flow diagrams
    5. The gains calendar
    6. One thing worth a look (tech at 53%)

View Demo shows the same cards one at a time, moving sideways with Back/Next buttons and arrow keys, plus a scripted question box on the last card.

  18. Stage 12 · Feedback on v10 · review of v01–v10

    One card at a time is too restrictive; related information should be seen together

    Your review of v10: you're not drawn to it. The one-card-at-a-time format feels too restrictive for this product, since you'd prefer to see related financial information together. v10 stays in the gallery.

    Your request for v11 onward: after ten versions, five new directions that are substantially different in layout and information organization, not the same dashboard structure in a new style. Be more creative and take bigger design risks, with unexpected ways to introduce the product and organize the information, while still making sense to users. You'll choose one to explore.

  19. Stage 12 · Decision → v11

    Five bolder directions proposed; “The Money Desk” chosen

    Five directions proposed, each a different basic form for the page: The Money Desk (organized in space), The Checkup (vital signs against your normal range), The Diff (last month vs. this month, line by line), The What-If Lab (levers and live outcomes) and Money Weather (conditions and forecast). You chose The Money Desk for v11.

    How v11 responds: no grid, columns or panels. Accounts are physical objects on a pannable, zoomable desk, placed so related things sit together: the pay stub next to the checking ledger, checking next to the savings jar, the card statement under checking, the two brokerage folders side by side under the desk calendar. Seven AI sticky notes are pinned between the objects they explain, with string to each. Examples: “paycheck in, rent and card bill out,” “September's savings transfer was skipped,” and “tech is 53% across both folders,” which strings to MSFT, AAPL, QQQ and NVDA. Clicking a note lights up everything it ties together; clicking an object lights up its notes. The v01-style picker sits above as a tray. Unticking an account removes its objects and notes, and the net-worth tape recalculates. The demo adds “Ask the desk,” which zooms to the matching note. On small screens the desk becomes a stacked drawer, with each note placed after the object it's about.

  20. Stage 13 · Feedback on v11 → v12

    Keep the desk direction, with a clearer starting point; try The Checkup next

    Your review of v11: you find it really creative and like the desk metaphor, but you're not sure it's practical yet: the information feels scattered and some of the text is small.

    What you'd change if the desk is developed further: keep the direction, but give users a clearer starting point; make the account overview and the most important changes stand out; let people open individual items for a larger, easier-to-read view.

    Your request: move on to direction 2, “The Checkup: organized by vital signs,” for v12.

  21. Stage 13 · Decision → v12

    “The Checkup” built, applying the v11 feedback

    How v12 responds: the page is organized by six vital signs instead of accounts, time or flows. Each sign combines bank and brokerage data and is drawn against your own usual range (shaded band from April–August, past months as dots, now as a large marker):

    • Cash cushion: 6.2 months
    • Saving rate: 25%
    • Spending vs. usual: −3%, with dining +18% called out
    • Bills ahead: 2.5×
    • Portfolio mix: 52.8% tech, above the usual 48.5–52.5%
    • Daily swings: 0.35% a day

    Your v11 feedback carried in:

    • A clear starting point: a header summary and four headline numbers.
    • The most important change first: out-of-range signs are sorted to the front and highlighted.
    • Readable text.
    • A larger view: each sign opens into a detail panel with its accounts, a monthly chart or category breakdown, and a fuller AI note.

    The gains calendar sits beside an “investment heartbeat” trace of September's daily moves. The tone stays descriptive (“notes, not advice”). The demo adds a scripted ask box that opens the matching sign.

  22. Stage 14 · Feedback on v12 → v13

    The Checkup isn't the one; try The Diff next

    Your review of v12: you're not very drawn to “The Checkup.” Keep v12 in the gallery as part of the exploration, but move on to another direction for v13 rather than refining this one.

    Your request: v13 tries “The Diff: organized by what changed, line by line.”

  23. Stage 14 · Decision → v13

    “The Diff” built

    How v13 responds: instead of a dashboard, the page is one comparison document across all accounts, borrowing the way programmers review changes between two versions of a file.

    • Two comparisons: “September vs. August” and “Since you last checked.” The second follows your earlier interest in the v08 summary.
    • Three sections: Cash, Money in & out, and Investments (each account and holding), with before and after values, the change, and red/green highlighting.
    • Unchanged lines fold away (“⋯ 2 unchanged lines: Income, Housing”) but can be opened.
    • AI comments sit like review comments on the lines worth explaining. Two are flagged as worth a look: dining up $114, and the September savings transfer that went from $500 to $0.
    • Top summary: a plain-language summary of changes, line counts and the net-worth difference give a clear starting point.
    • Gains calendar: shown as two months side by side.
    • Navigation: “Previous / Next change” buttons jump between changed lines.
    • Phones: switch to a unified view (old line, then new line).

    Tech share is computed, not scripted, and in this mock data it held steady (52.9%), so it is folded as unchanged rather than claimed as a change. The demo adds a scripted “find a line” box and n/p keyboard shortcuts.

  24. Stage 15 · Feedback on v13 → v14

    Comparing what changed is useful; a code-review look is wrong for this audience

    Your review of v13: you like the idea of comparing what changed, but the presentation feels too much like a GitHub diff. The target users want to understand their finances, and we shouldn't assume they're familiar with coding tools. v13 stays in the gallery with that distinction recorded: the comparison idea is useful; this presentation doesn't fit the audience.

    Your request for v14: move on to another direction, The What-If Lab, keeping the target users in mind and making the experience understandable without technical knowledge.

  25. Stage 15 · Decision → v14

    “The What-If Lab” built, in everyday language

    How v14 responds: the visitor tries what-ifs instead of reading a dashboard.

    • Switches and sliders, grouped plainly as “Everyday money,” “Coming up,” “Investing” and “The stock market.” Each lever says what it does in a sentence.
    • One results panel updates live: net worth before and after, checking, savings, investments, card balance owed and cash cushion, plus a “where your money sits” bar.
    • A plain-English explanation of why things changed. For example: “Restarting the savings transfer moves $500 from checking to savings. Your total doesn't change,” or, for a 10% market drop, which holdings move the most dollars and which tend to swing more than the market.
    • Starting point: “Where you stand today,” plus ready-made what-ifs (back to usual habits, next payday, a rough month for stocks, tech companies fall 15%, reset).
    • Scale: “What really happened” puts the September gains calendar beside the what-ifs.

    Following your v13 note: no technical vocabulary. Holdings are described by name (“Nvidia,” “a fund of the whole US market”), and every result is labeled an illustration, not a prediction or advice. The demo adds a scripted “What if…” box (e.g. “what if stocks fall 15%?”).

Plain HTML/CSS/JS, with no frameworks, build step or external APIs. All financial data is fictional. Schwab and IBKR are named only to label mock accounts.
